Why does the second derivative of the KL divergence happen to be a Riemannian metric? I know the proof. I have written the proof. I have explained the proof to a first-year. It still feels like a coincidence.

Maybe the right framing is: it is not a coincidence, it is a definition. The KL divergence is the unique (up to scale) f-divergence whose Hessian is invariant under sufficient statistics, and that Hessian is what we call the Fisher metric.
