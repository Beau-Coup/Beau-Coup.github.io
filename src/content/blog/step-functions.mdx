It turns out you can approximate functions by step functions. 
Let's make this rigorous with measure theory. 
These are my notes of chapter 3B of Axler's MIRA.

## Notation

We refer to the space of absolutely integrable real functions under the standard Lebesgue measure $\lambda$ by $\mathcal{L}^1(\textbf{R})$.
The norm $\|f\|_1$ denotes the integral of the abosulte value of $f$ with respect to $\lambda$:
$$
\|f\|_1 = \int |f| d \lambda.
$$

## Step Functions

A step function is a function that takes on finitely many values, each on a union of bounded intervals of the real line.
Formally:

<Definition term="Step function">
    A *step function* is a function $g: \mathbf{R} \to \mathbf{R}$ of the form
    $$
        g = a_1 \chi_{I_1} + \cdots + a_n \chi_{I_n},
    $$
    where $I_1, \dotsc , I_n$ are intervals of $\mathbf{R}$ and $a_1, \dotsc a_n$ are nonzero real numbers.
</Definition>

These will prove to be useful tools when discussion the space $\mathcal{L}^1(\mathbf{R})$ because integrals of step functions are straight-forward.
If $g$ is a step function, as above, and the intervals $I_1, \dotsc, I_n$ are disjoint, then 
$$
\| g\|_1 = |a_1| |I_1| + \cdots + |a_n|  |I_n|.
$$
In particular, $g \in \mathcal{L}^1(\mathbf{R})$ if, and only if, all the intervals $I_1, \dotsc, I_n$ are bounded.

We can approximate functions in $\mathcal{L}^1(\mathbf{R})$ by step functions.

<Proposition type="proposition" term="Approximation by step functions">
    Suppose $f \in \mathcal{L}^1(\mathbf{R})$. 
    Then for every $\varepsilon > 0 $, there exists a step function  
    $g \in \mathcal{L}^1(\mathbf{R})$ such that 
    $$
        \|f - g\|_1 < \varepsilon.
    $$
</Proposition>

While this is nice, it really is just a necessary step in proving the following theorem.
Recall Luzin's theorem, which gives a method of approximating a Borel measurable function by a continuous function.
While spectacular, the following result is usually more useful.

<Proposition type="proposition" term="Approximation by continuous functions">
    Suppose $f \in \mathcal{L}^1(\mathbf{R})$. 
    Then for every $\varepsilon > 0 $, there exists a continuous function $g: \mathbf{R} \to \mathbf{R}$
    such that 
    $$
        \|f - g\|_1 < \varepsilon, 
    $$
    and $\{x \in \mathbf{R}: g(x) \neq 0 \}$ is a bounded set.
</Proposition>
The sketch of the proof is that, as above, we can approximate $f$ using step functions. 
And, for each of those steps, we can approximate it arbitrarily well with a continuous function that looks like a trapezoid.
Then, $g$ is the sum of all those continuous functions, and the triangle inequality bounds the total error.
